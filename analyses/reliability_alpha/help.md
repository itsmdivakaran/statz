# Scale Reliability (Cronbach's α)

Measures the internal consistency of a set of items that are meant to form one scale: how closely the items vary together.

**Assumptions**
- All items measure the same construct and are scored in the same direction (reverse-score negatively worded items).
- Items are at least ordinal with several response categories.
- Alpha assumes equal loadings (tau-equivalence); it underestimates reliability when loadings differ.

**Interpreting the output**
- α of .70 or higher is usually called acceptable, .80 good, .90 excellent. Very high values (above .95) can mean redundant items.
- *Item-rest r* is the correlation of an item with the sum of the other items; values below .30 or negative values flag weak or reversed items.
- *α if item dropped* shows whether removing an item would raise reliability.

**References**
- Cronbach, L. J. (1951). Coefficient alpha and the internal structure of tests. *Psychometrika, 16*(3), 297–334.
- Feldt, L. S., Woodruff, D. J., & Salih, F. A. (1987). Statistical inference for coefficient alpha. *Applied Psychological Measurement, 11*(1), 93–103.
