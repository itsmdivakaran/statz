# Cohen's Kappa

Measures agreement between two raters who sort the same cases into categories, corrected for the agreement expected by chance.

**Assumptions**
- The two raters rate the same cases independently.
- Both raters use the same set of categories.
- Use weighted kappa when the categories are ordered, so that near-misses count as partial agreement.

**Interpreting the output**
- κ = 1 is perfect agreement and 0 is chance-level agreement. Common labels: up to .20 slight, .21 to .40 fair, .41 to .60 moderate, .61 to .80 substantial, above .80 almost perfect (Landis & Koch, 1977).
- Kappa depends on how common each category is; report the observed agreement alongside it.

**References**
- Cohen, J. (1960). A coefficient of agreement for nominal scales. *Educational and Psychological Measurement, 20*(1), 37–46.
- Cohen, J. (1968). Weighted kappa. *Psychological Bulletin, 70*(4), 213–220.
- Landis, J. R., & Koch, G. G. (1977). The measurement of observer agreement for categorical data. *Biometrics, 33*(1), 159–174.
